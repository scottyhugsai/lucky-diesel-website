## 0. The three highest-leverage things

**1 — Resolve the domain collision before anything else ships.**
`luckydiesel.com` is not a parked domain waiting for this site. It is a **live, fully
crawlable Shopify storefront** — 76 products, 29 collections, 2 pages, `Allow: /`, full
sitemap index (OBSERVED via `https://luckydiesel.com/sitemap.xml`). Canonicals, the
`/store` tree, `CANONICAL_NAP.website`, the 301 map and which hostname earns the entity
all depend on a decision that has not been made. Meanwhile the repo is building a
**second** catalogue at `/store/products/[handle]` that duplicates the Shopify one, on
the same intended hostname. See [§7.1](#71-the-domain-collision).

**2 — Street address, hours, and a verified Google Business Profile.**
Not a checklist item — for a local service business it is *the* determinant. The best
measured local study available finds that for **local-intent** queries Google shows a
**local pack 93% of the time and an AI Overview only 15%** of the time
([Whitespark, 540 queries, 3 cities, 6 verticals](https://whitespark.ca/blog/case-study-the-prevalence-of-ai-overviews-in-local-search/)).
The money queries still land in the pack. Google's `LocalBusiness` docs list `address`
as **required** and `geo` / `openingHoursSpecification` / `priceRange` as recommended
([source](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/structured-data/local-business)).
The competitor two towns over already ships all of it ([§7.6](#76-what-the-competitor-two-towns-over-is-doing)).
**Owner-blocked. Chase it hardest.** And per the box above, it is also the entity
disambiguation fix.

**3 — Win the symptom layer, and give the engine codes real URLs.**
Independent shops with no national brand are ranking page 1 *nationally* on diesel
symptom queries — live SERPs OBSERVED today ([§4.3](#43-symptom--job-to-be-done-queries--the-real-opening)).
Meanwhile Lucky Diesel's entire engine-code layer — **19 named generations** in
`lib/site.ts` (LB7, LLY, LBZ, LMM, LML, L5P, 7.3, 6.0, 6.4, three 6.7 PSD ranges, 12v,
24v, common rail, three 6.7 Cummins ranges) — has **no URLs at all**.
`app/(site)/[platform]/page.tsx` renders them as cards linking to
`/store/products?platform=…&gen=…`, and `app/robots.ts` disallows `/*?*`. The whole
layer is crawl-blocked. See [§7.3](#73-the-engine-code-layer-is-crawl-blocked).

### The two deliberate omissions — both correct, and pressure-tested

**`FAQPage` removal: correct, and the date in the code comment is exactly right.**
`lib/marketing/content/seo.ts` retires `faqSchema()` to a no-op, citing May 2026.
Confirmed against Google's changelog, which has two entries:

> **Deprecating the FAQ rich result feature** — "This feature will no longer appear in
> Google Search starting **May 7, 2026**." *(changelog entry, May 2026)*

> **Removing documentation for the FAQ rich result feature** — "The FAQ rich result
> feature is no longer shown in Google Search results." *(June 2026)*

The docs page now **301-redirects** to
`developers.google.com/search/updates#removing-faq-rich-result`, and FAQ is absent from
the current [structured data gallery](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/structured-data/search-gallery).
Support was dropped from the Rich Results Test in June 2026 and from the Search Console
API in August 2026. *(HowTo went the same way on 2023-09-14.)*

Three distinct things, worth keeping straight: **`FAQPage` as a schema.org type still
exists** and was never deprecated by schema.org; **Google's FAQ rich result** is dead;
**Google's FAQPage documentation** is deleted. Google's standing position on leaving
existing markup in place ([source](https://developers.google.com/search/blog/2023/08/howto-faq-changes)):
*"Structured data that's not being used does not cause problems for Search, but also has
no visible effects in Google Search."* So the no-op is the right call and there is nothing
to clean up.

⚠️ The local competitor `lowcountrydiag.com` **still ships `FAQPage` markup** (OBSERVED
today). It is doing the outdated thing. Do not copy it.

🔴 **And do not "fix" this by reaching for `QAPage`.** Google's `QAPage` content
guidelines name this exact substitution as an **invalid use case**:

> "**Don't use `QAPage` markup for content that has only one answer for a given question
> with no way for users to add alternative answers.**" … Invalid: "**An FAQ page written
> by the site itself with no way for users to submit alternative answers**" … "**Don't use
> `QAPage` markup for FAQ pages or pages where there are multiple questions per page.**"
> — [QAPage docs](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/structured-data/qapage)

Misusing it risks a structured-data manual action, which is strictly worse than no markup.
→ **Worth a comment in `seo.ts` next to the `faqSchema` no-op**, because "FAQPage is dead,
use QAPage instead" is exactly the plausible-sounding thing a future contributor will try.

**`AggregateRating` omission: correct decision — with one clause of the rationale
overstated.**

Google's `LocalBusiness` docs list `aggregateRating` and `review` as *"only recommended
for sites that capture reviews about **other** local businesses"*
([source](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/structured-data/local-business)),
and the review-snippet policy is explicit:

> "**If the entity that's being reviewed controls the reviews about itself, their pages
> that use `LocalBusiness` or any other type of `Organization` structured data are
> ineligible for star review feature.** … For example, a review about entity A is placed
> on the website of entity A, either directly in their structured data or **through an
> embedded third-party widget** (for example, Google Business reviews or Facebook reviews
> widget)."
> — [Review snippet guidelines](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/structured-data/review-snippet)

`AutoRepair` is a `LocalBusiness` subtype, so it inherits this. The code comment in
`seo.ts` is right that it is ineligible and right that the rule reaches embedded widgets.

**But the comment also calls it "a spam-policy risk", and Google's own FAQ is narrower
than that** ([2019 announcement](https://developers.google.com/search/blog/2019/09/making-review-rich-results-more-helpful)):

> *"Will I get a manual action for having self-serving reviews on my site?"* — "**You won't
> get a manual action just for this.**"
> *"Do I need to remove self-serving reviews…?"* — "**No, you don't need to remove them.**
> Google Search just won't display review snippets for those pages anymore."
> *"Does this update apply to `AggregateRating` too?"* — "**Yes. It applies to `Review` and
> `AggregateRating`.**"

So the accurate framing is **display suppression, not a penalty**. That does not change
the decision — the site has zero reviews, there is nothing to mark up, and adding dead
weight is pointless — but the code comment should say "ineligible and pointless" rather
than "spam-policy risk", so nobody later discovers the softer truth and concludes the
whole decision was wrong. **Keep the omission; tighten the comment.**

🟢 **One genuine opportunity this uncovers, for later.** The restriction is scoped to
`LocalBusiness`/`Organization`. **`aggregateRating` on `Product` is eligible** — and this
repo already emits `Product` schema on 76 parts pages. If the shop ever collects real
ratings on individual parts, from users, not aggregated from other sites, not incentivised
without disclosure, those *can* legitimately produce stars. Note the 2025 tightening:
Google recommends *"only accepting ratings that are accompanied by a review comment and
author's name."* Park it under [§9(c)](#c-longer-term); do not build it before there are
real ratings.

## 2. The 2026 search reality

### 2.1 What Google actually says — and it cuts against most of what the industry sells

Google published a dedicated guide in May 2026:
**[Optimizing your website for generative AI features on Google Search](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/ai-optimization-guide)**
(announced [2026-05-15](https://developers.google.com/search/blog/2026/05/a-new-resource-for-optimizing)).
It is now the canonical document, alongside
[AI features and your website](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/ai-features)
and the May 2025 post
[Top ways to ensure your content performs well in Google's AI experiences](https://developers.google.com/search/blog/2025/05/succeeding-in-ai-search).

**OFFICIAL**, on the mechanism:

> "Retrieval-augmented generation (RAG): A technique (also known as grounding) used to
> improve the quality, accuracy, and freshness of AI responses by relying on **our core
> Search ranking systems** to retrieve relevant, up-to-date web pages from our Search
> index."

> "The best practices for SEO continue to be relevant because our generative AI features
> on Google Search are **rooted in our core Search ranking and quality systems**."

**OFFICIAL**, on eligibility:

> "To be eligible to be shown in generative AI features on Google Search, a page must be
> **indexed and eligible to be shown in Google Search with a snippet**, fulfilling the
> Search technical requirements."

**OFFICIAL**, from the guide's *"Mythbusting generative AI search: what you don't need to
do"* section:

> "**Overfocusing on structured data:** Structured data **isn't required** for generative
> AI search, and there's **no special schema.org markup you need to add**. However, it's a
> good idea to continue using it as part of your overall SEO strategy, as it helps with
> being eligible for rich results on Google Search."

> "**LLMS.txt files and other 'special' markup:** You don't need to create new machine
> readable files, AI text files, markup, or Markdown to appear in Google Search
> (including its generative AI capabilities), as **Google Search itself doesn't use
> them**."

**The honest read for this project.** AI eligibility is ordinary indexing plus ordinary
quality, grounded on the ordinary Search index. That is a *hard constraint*, not a
loophole: **a brand-new zero-authority domain will not be retrieved by any of these
systems until it earns conventional ranking.** There is no AI shortcut. Everything in
[§4](#4-keyword-landscape) and [§6](#6-content-architecture) is therefore the AI strategy
as well as the SEO strategy.

### 2.2 The one genuinely new, actionable lever: the Search Console generative-AI control

**OFFICIAL** — [Search generative AI control](https://support.google.com/webmasters/answer/16908024?hl=en):

> "Note: As of **August 31, 2026**, we've rolled out this control to all websites
> worldwide."

Located at **Search Console → Settings → Search generative AI**, with three states:
*Include* (the default for all properties), *Exclude* (*"You won't receive any traffic or
impressions from these features"*), and *Inherit control from parent*.

> "This control **isn't used as a ranking or inclusion signal** affecting other parts of
> Search… This control **doesn't affect AI training**; to limit training… use
> Google-Extended."

**The operational trap is inheritance.** A URL-prefix property inherits from its closest
configured parent, defaulting up to the top-level domain property. If anyone has ever set
*Exclude* on a parent, a child inherits it silently. Given [§7.1](#71-the-domain-collision) —
where a Shopify property and a Next.js property may both exist for `luckydiesel.com` —
**this must be checked on every property on the day the domain is verified.** Changes take
1–2 days to propagate. Cost: zero. It is a hard eligibility gate.

### 2.3 Query fan-out — and the trap Google explicitly names

**OFFICIAL** — Elizabeth Reid, VP/Head of Search,
[Google blog, 2025-05-20](https://blog.google/products/search/google-search-ai-mode-update/):

> "Under the hood, AI Mode uses our **query fan-out technique**, breaking down your
> question into subtopics and issuing a **multitude of queries simultaneously** on your
> behalf."

The 2026 guide's worked example is, conveniently, a local-service query: *"how to fix a
lawn that's full of weeds"* fans out to *"best herbicides for lawns"*, *"remove weeds
without chemicals"*, *"how to prevent weeds in lawn"*.

**This is an argument for the symptom-page strategy** — a page that answers one narrow
sub-question well can be retrieved for a broader question it never targeted.

**But Google names the obvious abuse in the same document:**

> "While it might be tempting to create separate content for every possible variation of
> how people might search (for example, by focusing on other queries that people have
> asked, **or fan-out queries**), doing so primarily to manipulate rankings or generative
> AI responses in Google Search **violates Google's scaled content abuse spam policy**."

There is a cottage industry selling "fan-out query mapping". Google has named that
practice as spam-policy-adjacent. **Do not build Lucky Diesel 171 `platform × generation ×
service` pages on this rationale** ([§6.2](#62-where-programmatic-becomes-a-liability)).

### 2.4 Structured data and AI: the evidence points *away* from the hype

I went looking for evidence that schema drives AI citation, and what exists points the
other way.

**STUDY (Tier 1 — the only causal design I found)** —
[Ahrefs, 2026-05-11](https://ahrefs.com/blog/schema-ai-citations/). **1,885 pages** that
went from no JSON-LD to having JSON-LD (Aug 2025 – Mar 2026), matched against **~4,000
control URLs** on other domains with similar pre-period citation levels. Two-sample
t-tests, difference-in-differences, event-study plots, symmetrical-window re-analysis.

| Surface | Effect of adding schema |
|---|---|
| Google AI Overviews | **−4.6%** (statistically significant *decline*) |
| Google AI Mode | +2.4% (indistinguishable from zero) |
| ChatGPT | +2.2% (indistinguishable from zero) |

Ahrefs states the limitations and they are real: every treated page already had 100+ AIO
citations, so this tests schema's effect on pages *already in the consideration set*, not
on discovery; all schema types pooled; 30-day window; the AIO decline is unexplained. It
is also vendor-published.

**What I take from it for this repo:** ship correct `LocalBusiness`/`AutoRepair` schema
because it is cheap, because it is required for rich-result eligibility, and above all
because it is how you state your NAP, hours and `@id` unambiguously — the entity work in
[§7.5](#75-the-product-schema-is-not-gated-on-the-claims-check) and
[§7.4](#74-the-schema-graph-has-no-identity). **Do not sell it internally as an AI
visibility lever.** There is no evidence for that and one decent causal study pointing
mildly the other way.

### 2.5 Local search in the AI era — the finding that should shape strategy

**STUDY (Tier 2, and the most decision-relevant item in this section)** —
[Whitespark, Miriam Ellis, 2025-05-12](https://whitespark.ca/blog/case-study-the-prevalence-of-ai-overviews-in-local-search/).
540 queries · Houston / Phoenix / Denver · 6 verticals (plumbers, PI lawyers, dentists,
optometrists, medical clinics, realtors) · classified by intent.

| Query intent | AI Overview appears | Local pack appears |
|---|---|---|
| **Local intent** (*"plumber near me"*) | **15%** | **93%** |
| Informational | 92% | 6% |
| Hybrid | 97% | 17% |
| Overall | 68% | 39% |

> "There is no apparent correlation between local pack and AI Overview prominence."

**AI Overviews and the local pack are complementary surfaces split by intent, not
competitors.** The high-commercial-intent queries that generate phone calls for a shop
still overwhelmingly return a local pack. The AI surface owns the research end.

Caveats, stated honestly: the study is ~16 months old, AIO trigger rates have moved, and
the researchers were not physically in-market. But the *intent split* is the durable
finding and it is why [§0](#0-the-three-highest-leverage-things) item 2 (GBP) outranks
everything AI-flavoured.

**Google's own position on GBP and AI is consistent with this** — OFFICIAL, 2026 guide:

> "Using products like Merchant Center (such as Merchant Center feeds) and **Google
> Business Profiles can help your products and services to be visible in both AI
> responses and other Google Search results**."

**One more converging signal.** [Seer Interactive](https://www.seerinteractive.com/insights/study-of-800k-ai-responses-how-reviews-shape-brand-presence-in-ai-search)
(804,491 AI responses, 1,926 brands, 15,783 prompts, March 2026) found that **99.5% of
review-site citations in AI answers arrived via organic search ranking, not direct
AI-platform requests.** I am sceptical of that study's headline "1% → 53.5%" jump — the
matching almost certainly does not remove selection effects, and the whole piece is framed
around Trustpilot — but the 99.5% mechanism finding is its most defensible result, and it
says the same thing as everything else here: **AI citation is downstream of classic
ranking.**

### 2.6 What actually makes *this* business citable — INFERENCE, reasoned

Google declines to give an AI-specific recipe, so this is derived. In confidence order:

1. **Entity clarity — highest value available, and now urgent.** See the box at the top of
   this document. A brand-new business with an older namesake that has an `epa.gov`
   enforcement PDF, no address, no GBP and no reviews is an ambiguous entity with a
   negative-signal collision. Everything that disambiguates — street address, verified
   GBP, consistent NAP, a stable schema `@id`, "Charleston" adjacent to the name
   everywhere — is the work. The repo already instinctively pairs `BUSINESS.name` with
   the city in nearly every generated title; that instinct is correct and should be
   extended into the schema graph.
2. **Extractable facts.** Answer engines lift discrete stated facts. *"We work on LB7
   through L5P"*, *"we quote before any work starts"*, *"we do not remove emissions
   equipment"*, *"parts from $444"* are extractable. Atmosphere is not. The `SERVICES`
   blurbs in `lib/site.ts` are good at this.
3. **Consistent NAP.** `auditNap()` in `lib/marketing/content/seo.ts` already implements
   this properly, including treating a missing address on both sides as a non-mismatch.
   Note `CANONICAL_NAP.website` is hardcoded to `https://luckydiesel.com` — currently the
   *Shopify* store. Correct or badly wrong depending on [§7.1](#71-the-domain-collision).
4. **First-hand expertise.** Google's own contrast in the 2026 guide is instructive:
   *"7 Tips for First-Time Homebuyers"* (commodity, worthless) versus *"Why We Waived the
   Inspection & Saved Money: A Look Inside the Sewer Line"* (first-hand, specific,
   citable). For a shop with nothing to photograph, the only honest first-hand signal is
   **diagnostic** experience: here is the code, here is what it actually turned out to be,
   here is how we told the difference. That costs nothing but the owner's time, and it is
   exactly what the shops ranking nationally are doing
   ([§4.3](#43-symptom--job-to-be-done-queries--the-real-opening)).

### 2.7 Non-Google assistants: which bot you block matters

**OFFICIAL** — [OpenAI bots documentation](https://developers.openai.com/api/docs/bots):

| Bot | Purpose | Blocking it costs you |
|---|---|---|
| **OAI-SearchBot** | *"used to surface websites in **search results in ChatGPT's search features**"* | **Citations in ChatGPT search.** This is the expensive one |
| **GPTBot** | training foundation models | Nothing citation-wise |
| **ChatGPT-User** | user-initiated page fetches | Live fetches during a user's session |

OpenAI is explicit that these are separable:

> "**Each setting is independent of the others** – for example, a webmaster can allow
> OAI-SearchBot in order to appear in search results while disallowing GPTBot to indicate
> that crawled content should not be used for training…"

Anthropic runs the same three-way split — **ClaudeBot** (training), **Claude-User**
(user-initiated), **Claude-SearchBot** (search index)
([source](https://support.claude.com/en/articles/8896518-does-anthropic-crawl-data-from-the-web-and-how-can-site-owners-block-the-crawler)).
Perplexity runs **PerplexityBot** (search, respects robots.txt) and **Perplexity-User**
(*"this fetcher generally ignores robots.txt rules"*)
([source](https://docs.perplexity.ai/docs/resources/perplexity-crawlers)).

⚠️ Perplexity's documented posture should not be taken as a description of its behaviour:
Cloudflare
[documented undeclared stealth crawlers](https://blog.cloudflare.com/perplexity-is-using-stealth-undeclared-crawlers-to-evade-website-no-crawl-directives/)
impersonating Chrome on never-indexed domains with `Disallow: /`, and de-listed Perplexity
as a verified bot. Perplexity disputes it.

**For this repo:** `app/robots.ts` uses a single `userAgent: '*'` rule, so all of these
inherit `Disallow: ['/portal', '/shop', '/admin', '/login', '/api/', '/*?*']`. That is
fine as far as it goes — but it means every AI search crawler also loses the store's
faceted navigation, which compounds [§7.3](#73-the-engine-code-layer-is-crawl-blocked).
Also audit the Vercel firewall / any WAF bot-management rules: a managed rule silently
blocking `OAI-SearchBot` is a common and invisible failure.

### 2.8 The genuinely new thing: Shopify's agent surface is already live on luckydiesel.com

**OBSERVED today**, and not recorded in `docs/DISCOVERY.md`:

### 2.9 `llms.txt` — do not build it

**OFFICIAL — John Mueller,
[Bluesky, 2025-06-17](https://bsky.app/profile/johnmu.com/post/3lrshm4gggs2v):**

> "FWIW no AI system currently uses llms.txt."

**OFFICIAL — 2026 guide**, quoted in [§2.1](#21-what-google-actually-says--and-it-cuts-against-most-of-what-the-industry-sells),
plus its Next-steps recap: *"you can ignore tactics like 'chunking' content, **creating
unnecessary AI text files (like llms.txt)**, or pursuing inauthentic mentions."*

Neither OpenAI nor Anthropic has documented reading it in any retrieval pipeline.

**STUDY** — [Casey Burridge, 2026-06-20](https://caseyrb.com/blog/state-of-llms-txt-adoption/),
using HTTP Archive's BigQuery `llms_txt_validation` metric: **5.61%** of the top 10,000
sites serve a valid `llms.txt`, up from 1.04% a year earlier — but **Shopify alone hit
78.1%** after a silent platform-wide rollout in April–May 2026. So most of the "adoption"
curve is one vendor flipping a switch.

Which means: **`luckydiesel.com` almost certainly already has an `llms.txt`, courtesy of
Shopify, and it has changed nothing.**

**Verdict: vendor hype. Do not add it to this repo.** Cost to ship is an hour so it is not
harmful, but it must not be sold to anyone as an AI-visibility tactic.

## 5. The emissions problem

> ⚖️ **Not legal advice.** This section is research for a lawyer to act on. It is the
> highest-stakes material in this document.

### 5.1 The statute, in the words that matter

**OFFICIAL — 42 U.S.C. §7522(a)(3)(B)**, verbatim
([govinfo](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USCODE-2013-title42/html/USCODE-2013-title42-chap85-subchapII-partA-sec7522.htm)):

> "for any person to **manufacture or sell, or offer to sell, or install**, any part or
> component intended for use with, or as part of, any motor vehicle or motor vehicle
> engine, where **a principal effect** of the part or component is to bypass, defeat, or
> render inoperative any device or element of design installed on or in a motor vehicle
> … and where **the person knows or should know** that such part or component is being
> offered for sale or installed for such use or put to such use"

**Does it cover advertising?** The word "advertise" does not appear in §7522. But that is
not the end of it, and this is the point an SEO document has to get right:

- The operative phrase is **"offer to sell."** Advertising a delete kit or delete service,
  with a price, to the public **is** the ordinary legal meaning of offering for sale. An
  ad is not a separate offence category — it *is*, or is direct evidence of, the
  prohibited act.
- The mental-state element is **"knows or should know."** Marketing copy is the best
  available evidence of knowledge and of intended use. A page saying "we delete DPF/EGR on
  6.7 Powerstrokes" proves both the offer and the scienter in one sentence.
- **"install" is in the statute.** A shop that sells nothing and only installs a
  customer-supplied kit is squarely liable. Installation-only is not a safe harbour.

**This is precisely why emissions is an SEO problem and not only a shop-floor problem: the
website is the discoverable, timestamped artefact.**

### 5.2 EPA reads websites, and orders them changed

This is the finding that should end any internal debate.

**OFFICIAL — [White's Diesel Performance settlement, announced 10 January 2025](https://www.epa.gov/newsreleases/epa-and-justice-department-reach-settlement-whites-diesel-performance-failure-respond).**
A Florida **installer shop**, not a manufacturer. 748+ defeat devices sold/installed, 46
vehicles tampered. Civil penalty only **$10,000**, expressly reduced for inability to pay.
The consent decree required the shop to **"revise its websites and marketing materials by
removing all references to, and photos of, tampering and defeat devices"** and to post a
public settlement statement on its own site.

**OFFICIAL — [Rudy's Performance Parts / Aaron Rudolf](https://www.epa.gov/enforcement/rudys-performance-parts-inc-and-aaron-rudolf-clean-air-act-settlement-information-sheet)**
(250,000+ products, $10M total). Consent decree: **"revise all marketing materials to
strike any information relating to replacing, overwriting, deleting, bypassing, defeating,
or rendering inoperative any emission control."**

**OFFICIAL — [Performance Diesel Inc.](https://www.epa.gov/newsreleases/settlement-epa-and-department-justice-prohibits-performance-diesel-inc-selling-diesel)**
— 5,549 defeat devices that were **electronic tunes**, $1.1M. Software tunes are defeat
devices on the same footing as hardware. Directly relevant to a shop whose
`SERVICES[0]` is tuning.

And, per the box at the top of this document, the **"Lucky Diesel Performance" expedited
settlement** shows the small-shop end of the same enforcement: three violations, $1,894,
evidenced by **two invoices**.

**Two operational conclusions:**
1. EPA reads shop websites, and website scrubbing is a standard remedy. Content published
   today is discoverable later. The civil statute of limitations is five years
   (28 U.S.C. §2462) ⚖️ — content published in 2026 remains actionable into 2031.
2. **Photos are evidence.** "references to, **and photos of**" is doing real work in that
   decree. This has a direct bearing on `docs/IMAGERY.md`: when real photography finally
   exists, **no image of removed emissions hardware, a deleted truck, or smoke may ever be
   published.** Add that to the imagery rules.

### 5.3 What actually changed in 2026 — and the trap in it

⚖️ **The claim in `docs/DISCOVERY.md` that "DOJ dropped criminal defeat-device cases Jan
2026" is TRUE, and it is the most dangerous fact in this document.**

**SECONDARY, multi-outlet corroborated** (I have not seen the memo itself):
a memorandum of **21 January 2026** signed by Deputy Attorney General **Todd Blanche**
directs prosecutors to drop every pending CAA defeat-device criminal case and open no new
ones — reportedly affecting 12+ active prosecutions and 20+ investigations.
([E&E News/POLITICO](https://www.eenews.net/articles/doj-ends-criminal-prosecutions-of-vehicle-emissions-defeat-devices/) ·
[CBS News](https://www.cbsnews.com/news/justice-department-auto-emissions-cheating-cases/) ·
[Cowboy State Daily](https://cowboystatedaily.com/2026/01/21/department-of-justice-halts-federal-diesel-delete-prosecutions/))

**But DOJ said, in the same breath:** *"In partnership with the EPA, DOJ will still pursue
**civil enforcement** for these violations when appropriate."*

**Why this is a trap and not a green light:**
1. The **statute is unchanged**. §7522(a)(3)(B) is fully in force.
2. **Civil liability is expressly preserved** — and civil is where essentially all
   small-shop enforcement lives anyway (172 civil vs 17 criminal cases FY2020–23).
3. DOJ policy is **discretionary and reversible** by the next memo or administration,
   while the **five-year civil limitations period** means today's content is live evidence
   into 2031. ⚖️
4. Blanche's legal theory is reportedly **contested by EPA's own attorneys**.
5. **Citizen suits** under CAA §304, and state enforcement, are untouched.

Relatedly, and correcting a framing that may be floating around: **EPA's defeat-device
National Enforcement and Compliance Initiative ran FY2020–FY2023 and was NOT renewed** for
FY2024–2027 ([current NECI list, updated 2026-03-09](https://www.epa.gov/enforcement/national-enforcement-and-compliance-initiatives)).
It reverted to core enforcement — fewer dedicated resources, same authority. *Not renewed*
≠ *not enforced*.

**Current civil penalty, verified at 40 C.F.R. §19.4:** for §7522(a)(3) violations by a
person **other than a manufacturer or dealer**, **$5,911 per violation** for penalties
assessed on or after 8 January 2025 (higher tier $59,114). Federal Register citation:
**90 FR 1375 (8 Jan 2025)**.
[eCFR](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-40/chapter-I/subchapter-A/part-19/section-19.4) ·
[FR](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2025/01/08/2025-00206/civil-monetary-penalty-inflation-adjustment).
**Penalties are per device / per violation**, so a shop that did 50 of something faces a
theoretical ~$295K exposure. ⚖️ Which tier a repair shop falls into turns on the defined
term "dealer" and drives a 10× difference — counsel must confirm.

### 5.5 "Off-road use only" is not a shield — EPA's own words

This is the most misunderstood item in the industry, so here is the actual text.

**OFFICIAL — [EPA Tampering Policy, 23 November 2020](https://www.epa.gov/sites/production/files/2020-12/documents/epatamperingpolicy-enforcementpolicyonvehicleandenginetampering.pdf)**, verbatim:

> "This Policy does not address vehicles, engines, or equipment that are excluded from the
> definitions of motor vehicle… For example, this Policy does not address vehicles
> originally built and used exclusively for competitive motor sports… **Also, this Policy
> does not address EPA-certified motor vehicles that are converted into a vehicle used
> solely for competition motorsports, nor aftermarket parts purportedly manufactured or
> sold for that purpose.**"

Parse it carefully. Vehicles **originally built** for competition fall outside the
definition of "motor vehicle". A certified road truck *converted* to racing, and parts
"**purportedly**" sold for that purpose, are **"not addressed"** — meaning *no protection
is offered*, not that they are exempt. "Purportedly" is doing deliberate work. The Policy
is also expressly nonbinding and "in no way affects the EPA's authority to investigate and
enforce."

In practice the disclaimer functions as **evidence the seller knew** the part defeats
emissions controls — i.e. it helps prove the statute's "knows or should know" element. The
**RPM Act**, which would create a competition exemption, **has never been enacted.**

**The repo already reaches the right conclusion.** `lib/marketing/core/compliance.ts`
blocks "race/off-road only" paired with tuning or street use, with the reason *"a known
tampering disclaimer that does not make it legal."* That is correct and well-sourced.
⚖️ But note the implication for the *store*: the AMDP SKU is tagged `Off-Road Use Only`
and the repo currently treats that tag as a *reason to exclude from promotion*. It should
also be read as a **flag for counsel**, not as a mitigation.

**OFFICIAL — EPA on what you may and may not claim** (Tampering Policy, note iii):

> "**No claims of any kind, such as 'Approved [or certified] by the Environmental
> Protection Agency,' may be made on the basis of this Policy. This includes written and
> oral advertisements and other communication.** However, if true on the basis of this
> Policy, statements such as the following may be made: **'Has no adverse effect on
> emissions, consistent with the EPA Tampering Policy (2019).'**"

**EPA has pre-approved a specific marketing sentence.** Use it verbatim where true and
documented; never say "EPA approved".

| **SEMA Certified-Emissions parts** | 2026 EPA advisory | See [§5.7](#57-sema-certified-emissions--the-repo-already-modelled-this-and-it-just-got-real) |
| **★ Delete reversal / restore to stock** | RB A — **expressly endorsed** | See below |

**★ Delete reversal is explicitly blessed by EPA.** From Appendix A of the Lucky Diesel
Performance settlement, verbatim:

> "Where a person is asked to perform service on an element of an emission control system
> that has already been tampered, the EPA typically does not consider the service to be
> illegal tampering if the person **either declines to perform the service on the tampered
> system or restores the element to its certified configuration.**"

**This should be a flagship page.** It is legal, EPA-endorsed, commercially timely given
[§5.3](#53-what-actually-changed-in-2026--and-the-trap-in-it), essentially uncontested in
the SERPs, and it positions the shop with the regulatory direction rather than against it.
There is a real supply of trucks in the Lowcountry that need putting back to stock.

**Two governing principles from the same Appendix A:**
- **Never modify any OBD system** — EPA's definition is expansive: any element that
  "monitors, measures, receives, reads, stores, reports, processes or transmits any
  information about the condition of or the performance of an emission control system."
- **Document a reasonable basis at or before the time the conduct occurs.** Not
  afterwards.

Note the operational detail that matters for a website: RB A permits the manufacturer's
written representation to live *"in a publicly available source such as a product
catalogue or **website**."* That is an argument for the repo's existing compliance model —
`COMPLIANCE_LABEL`, `normalizeEoNumber`, per-product status on the product page — being
shown publicly rather than kept in admin.

**The positioning that follows:** Lucky Diesel should be the Charleston shop that is
*loudly* good at making emissions systems work, not the one quietly known for removing
them. That is defensible, rankable, ad-eligible (no ad platform runs delete terms), and it
differentiates against every offshore dropshipper that owns the delete SERPs.

### 5.7 SEMA Certified-Emissions — the repo already modelled this, and it just got real

`lib/store/compliance.ts:11` defines `COMPLIANCE_STATUSES = ['carb_eo', 'sema_verified',
'unverified', 'not_applicable']`. That `sema_verified` value became materially more
valuable in mid-2026.

**SECONDARY — [SEMA, "What EPA recognition of SEMA Certified-Emissions means"](https://www.sema.org/news-media/enews/2026/30/faq-what-epa-recognition-sema-certified-emissions-means-aftermarket).**
EPA reportedly issued an advisory opinion (~1 July 2026) recognising **SEMA
Certified-Emissions (SC-E)** as satisfying the "documented reasonable basis" requirement
under the 2020 Tampering Policy — a **49-state** pathway, excluding California, where a
CARB EO is still required. ⚖️ Verify the advisory opinion text directly with SEMA before
citing the date publicly.

**Why this matters for a South Carolina shop:** it is a second documented compliance
pathway that does not require California paperwork. Combined with CARB EO (which EPA
accepts as a federal reasonable basis under RB E even though **SC is not a CARB state**),
the shop has two legitimate, marketable compliance credentials. The repo is already
structured to surface both.

⚠️ And a marketing caution the repo's claims checker already flags as a `warn`:
**"50-state legal" is application-specific.** An EO covers *listed vehicles only*,
installed per instructions. Never make it a blanket brand claim.

### 5.8 South Carolina: no inspection, but two statutes nobody mentions

**SC has no vehicle emissions inspection programme.** A full-text search of SC Code Title
56, Chapter 5 returns **zero occurrences of "emission"**. ⚖️ The first-party SCDMV page
404'd during this research, so confirm before publishing "no inspection required" as a
factual claim. (Also note **SC DHEC was reorganised into SC DES** — Department of
Environmental Services — in 2024; `docs/DISCOVERY.md` references "SC DHEC" and that name
is likely stale.)

The repo already encodes the trap as a **blocking** rule in
`lib/marketing/core/compliance.ts`:

```
{ rule: 'no_inspection', severity: 'block', …
  reason: 'Implies tampering is fine because SC has no emissions inspection. Federal law still applies.' }
```

Exactly right. Every enforcement case in [§5.2](#52-epa-reads-websites-and-orders-them-changed)
arose in a non-CARB, limited-I/M state (FL, NC, TN, NJ, OK). No inspection just means
nobody catches it at renewal; EPA catches it through invoices and websites.

**But two SC statutes are directly relevant and routinely overlooked**
([SC Code Title 56 Ch. 5](https://www.scstatehouse.gov/code/t56c005.php)):

> **§56-5-5020 (Mufflers):** "…to prevent excessive or unusual noise and **annoying
> smoke**… The engine and power mechanism of every motor vehicle shall be so equipped and
> adjusted as to **prevent the escape of excessive fumes and smoke**."

> **§56-5-5030:** "It shall be unlawful for any person to attach to any motor vehicle any
> device to emit from the exhaust pipe or any other part of such vehicle any **smoke
> screen, noisome gases or odors**… Any person violating… shall be guilty of a
> **misdemeanor**."

**INFERENCE** (no SC case law found applying these to diesel modification ⚖️): a visibly
smoking modified truck plausibly implicates §56-5-5020, and smoke-switch hardware
plausibly implicates §56-5-5030 as a misdemeanour.

**Practical rule, and it belongs in `docs/IMAGERY.md`: never publish smoke imagery.** It is
marketing evidence under federal law *and* potentially a state misdemeanour exhibit. The
repo's `rolling_coal` block rule already covers the words; the images rule needs writing
down too.

### 5.9 FTC — two claims to get right

**Substantiation.** Advertisers need a reasonable basis for express *and implied* claims
**before** dissemination
([FTC Policy Statement](https://www.ftc.gov/legal-library/browse/ftc-policy-statement-regarding-advertising-substantiation)).
FTC's automotive history is fuel-economy and additive claims (Dura Lube, Motor Up,
Prolong, Slick 50). I found **no FTC case specifically about horsepower claims on
aftermarket parts** — the doctrine plainly covers them, but the precedent is analogous
rather than on point.
→ The repo's `power_gain` `warn` rule (*"Power claims need real dyno data for this truck
and a compliant tune"*) is exactly the right instinct. Keep it.

**Magnuson-Moss / "aftermarket parts void your warranty"** —
[FTC](https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/blog/2019/03/nixing-fix-warranties-mag-moss-restrictions-repairs):

> "Companies can't void a consumer's warranty or deny warranty coverage **solely because**
> the consumer uses a part made by someone else… **A company may, however, disclaim
> warranty coverage for defects or damage caused by the use of unauthorized parts or
> service.**"

⚠️ **This cuts both ways.** "Aftermarket parts can't void your warranty!" is a popular shop
headline and it is an **overstatement** — itself potentially an FTC-actionable deceptive
claim. The accurate version: a manufacturer cannot void a warranty merely because an
aftermarket part was used; it *can* deny a claim for damage the part actually caused, and
it bears the burden of showing causation. **And it is irrelevant to deletes** — a delete
is unlawful regardless of warranty consequences. Never use Mag-Moss to soften delete
messaging.

### 5.10 Delete terms: do not build pages on them

OBSERVED SERPs for "6.7 Cummins EGR delete" / "LMM DPF delete" are wall-to-wall offshore
and dropship e-commerce: KSP Motor, SPELAB, Tuner Depot, Diesel Patriots, The Diesel
Dudes, Flashark, thedpfdelete.com, enginego.com. **Essentially no US brick-and-mortar shop
ranks** — which, given [§5.2](#52-epa-reads-websites-and-orders-them-changed), is not a
coincidence.

So the trade is: take the legal exposure of an indexed service page with the shop's own
address on it, forgo every paid channel, and still lose the SERP to sellers with no US
enforcement exposure. **There is no version of this that is a good deal.**

### 5.11 The do-not-publish register

🔴 **Never on the site, GBP, Instagram, Facebook, TikTok, or in a review response:**
1. DPF / EGR / DEF / SCR / cat **delete**, "delete kit", "delete tune", "EGR blocker",
   "DPF-back", "race pipe"
2. **Photos** of removed emissions hardware, deleted trucks, or **smoke** (White's Diesel
   decree: "references to, **and photos of**"; plus SC §56-5-5030)
3. "Off-road use only" / "race use only" **as a shield** — it is scienter evidence
4. **"EPA approved" / "EPA certified"** — expressly prohibited by the Tampering Policy
5. Blanket **"50-state legal"** without per-application EO numbers
6. Unsubstantiated **HP / torque / MPG** figures
7. **"Aftermarket parts can't void your warranty"** stated absolutely
8. Any implication that the **January 2026 DOJ memo** legalised deletes

The repo's `checkClaims()` already blocks 1, 3, 5 (as `warn`), 6 (as `warn`) and the
"no inspection" framing. **Items 2, 4, 7 and 8 are not currently covered** and should be
added — 2 to `docs/IMAGERY.md`, 4/7/8 as new rules in
`lib/marketing/core/compliance.ts`.

⚖️ **Off-site is in scope too.** EPA read websites in every case above, and invoices were
the evidence in the Lucky Diesel Performance settlement. The audit has to include social
profiles and the Shopify store, not just this repo.

## 6. Content architecture

The constraint is unusual: **no reviews, no builds, seven photographs.** The page types
that normally carry a shop — gallery, testimonials, before/after — are all empty.
`docs/IMAGERY.md` is admirably strict that the generated textures may never stand in for
documentary photography, and that rule extends to SEO: no schema `image` pointing at a
texture as though it depicted work performed.

### 6.1 What earns rankings here, ranked

**1. Symptom / diagnostic guides — hand-written, technician-voiced.** The only page type
with demonstrated evidence of working for zero-authority shops in this exact vertical
([§4.3](#43-symptom--job-to-be-done-queries--the-real-opening)). `draftJobBlogPost()` in
`lib/marketing/content/seo.ts` already generates the right shape — *symptom → what we
found → what we did → avoiding it* — from real job data, with phone numbers scrubbed.
That is the correct engine; it needs real jobs flowing through it.

**2. Emissions-service pages — the best-defended commercial opportunity in the report.**
See [§5.6](#56-what-the-shop-can-legitimately-rank-for). DPF cleaning, EGR cooler
replacement, DEF/SCR repair, regen diagnosis, and above all **delete reversal / back to
stock**. Legal, EPA-endorsed, ad-eligible, and — per §5.10 — in SERPs that no compliant US
shop is currently contesting.

**3. Engine-generation pages — 19, hand-written.** See
[§7.3](#73-the-engine-code-layer-is-crawl-blocked).

**4. Service pages — there are currently none.** `SERVICES` defines nine services and
every link to them is an anchor: `/?truck=duramax&service=tuning#quote`. Nine services
with real commercial intent, zero URLs. `/tuning` most of all, because
[§4.4](#44-local-queries--two-real-gaps-and-one-volume-trap) found that term unclaimed in
Charleston.

**5. Service-area pages — built, still placeholders, and that is the right state.**
`draftAreaPage()` emits `[Add the kinds of trucks and jobs you get from …]` and
`seo-guard.ts` refuses to publish while a `[Add …]` note remains.

The doorway rule is narrower than it is usually quoted, and the exact wording matters
([Google spam policies](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/essentials/spam-policies),
the section is now headed **"Doorway abuse"**, page updated 2026-08-28):

> "Doorway abuse is when sites or pages are created to rank for specific, similar search
> queries. **They lead users to intermediate pages that aren't as useful as the final
> destination.**" Examples include: "Having multiple domain names or pages targeted at
> specific regions or cities **that funnel users to one page**" and "Creating
> **substantially similar** pages that are closer to search results than a clearly
> defined, browseable hierarchy."

**Note the conjunction.** The second bullet has two conditions — targeted at cities *and*
funnelling to one page — and the opening sentence frames the defect as **intermediate**
pages. A city page that is *itself* the destination, where the visitor can read something
true and request a quote, does not meet that description. Google has never published
guidance saying "don't make city pages."

**But the bar is relative, not absolute.** Google's Quality Rater Guidelines (§4.6.5,
September 2025 edition, which Google links site owners to) put it more harshly than the
public policy does: scaled content *"provides little to no value for website visitors
**compared to other similar pages on the web**"*, and §4.6.6 defines the failure mode
precisely — *"We will consider MC to be paraphrased if it contains content that is
unoriginal and exists on other pages, **with different wording, organization or
phrasing**."* ⚠️ Rater ratings do not directly influence ranking; cite this as evidence of
intent, not as a mechanism.

**So for `/service-areas/[area]`:** six pages that are the destination, each with genuinely
different substance, are defensible. Six pages that are one template with the city swapped
are "paraphrased" by Google's own definition, whatever they funnel to. That is exactly
what `seo-guard.ts`'s `DUPLICATE_SIMILARITY = 0.6` Jaccard check is for. **Keep the
guard.**

**6. Builds / dyno pages — blocked on reality.** `/builds/[slug]` and `draftBuildPage()`
are well-designed (real before/after numbers, "Results vary"). They cannot ship until
there are real builds. Do not soften this.

### 6.2 Where programmatic becomes a liability

> "Scaled content abuse is when many pages are generated for the primary purpose of
> manipulating search rankings and not helping users." Examples include "Using generative
> AI tools or other similar tools to generate many pages without adding value for users"
> and "Creating many pages where the content makes little or no sense to a reader but
> contains search keywords."
> — [Google spam policies](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/essentials/spam-policies)

And, from [§2.3](#23-query-fan-out--and-the-trap-google-explicitly-names), building pages
per *fan-out query* is named in the same breath.

| Page set | Count | Verdict |
|---|---|---|
| 3 platform pages | 3 | ✅ genuinely distinct |
| 19 engine-generation pages | 19 | ✅ **if hand-written.** LB7 injectors ≠ LLY overheating ≠ LML CP4 — real, different failure modes. Templated with a variable swapped, ❌ |
| ~9 service pages | 9 | ✅ |
| 6 service-area pages | 6 | ⚠️ only with genuine local substance |
| `platform × generation × service` matrix | 171 | ❌ **doorway abuse.** Do not build |
| `/fitment?year=…&make=…` combinations | thousands | ✅ **already handled correctly** — `noindex, follow`, canonical to the platform page, with the reasoning in a code comment. The best single SEO decision in the repo |
| 76 `/store/products/[handle]` | 76 | ⚠️ depends entirely on [§7.1](#71-the-domain-collision) |

**Rule of thumb for this project:** a page is safe to generate if the owner could
personally add one true sentence to it that appears nowhere else on the site. If he can't,
it's paraphrase.

Two more things worth knowing here:

- **Scale is not the violation; purpose is.** Google, launching the policy
  ([2024-03-05](https://developers.google.com/search/blog/2024/03/core-update-spam-policies)):
  *"producing content at scale is abusive **if done for the purpose of manipulating search
  rankings**, and this applies whether automation or humans are involved."* The repo's
  `MONTHLY_PUBLISH_CAP = 8` is a self-imposed brake on exactly the failure mode.
- **Spam policy now explicitly reaches AI answers.** Changelog 2026-05-15: *"Clarified
  that spam policies apply to generative AI responses in Google Search."* Scaled-content
  exposure is no longer just a blue-links problem.
- **Auto-generated metadata is where programmatic builds usually fail.** Google:
  *"focus on accuracy, quality, and relevance, especially when automatically generating the
  content. **This includes metadata like `<title>` elements, meta description elements,
  structured data, and alternate texts for images.**"*
  ([source](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/using-gen-ai-content)).
  Relevant to `seoMetadata()` and to the `draft*` functions in
  `lib/marketing/content/seo.ts`, which generate titles and meta descriptions from
  templates — those need to vary as much as the bodies do, and `seo-guard.ts` currently
  only checks body text.

## 7. Technical SEO — what this repo is not doing

### 7.1 The domain collision

`luckydiesel.com` today serves a Shopify storefront: 76 products, 29 collections, 2 pages,
`Allow: /`, full sitemap index, and the agent surface from
[§2.8](#28-the-genuinely-new-thing-shopifys-agent-surface-is-already-live-on-luckydieselcom).
`lib/site.ts` sets `BUSINESS.store = 'https://luckydiesel.com'` and
`lib/marketing/content/seo.ts` sets `CANONICAL_NAP.website = 'https://luckydiesel.com'` —
so the repo already asserts the Shopify host is the canonical website, while building a
competing catalogue at `/store/products/[handle]`.

| Option | Consequence |
|---|---|
| **A.** Next.js at apex, Shopify to `shop.luckydiesel.com` | Needs a 301 map for ~108 URLs. Those pages hold whatever equity exists — the only ranking asset the business currently has. Splits the entity across two hostnames |
| **B.** Shopify keeps the apex, Next.js on `www.` or a path | Confusing; splits the entity worse |
| **C.** Next.js at apex, Shopify products proxied through it (which `lib/store/catalog.ts` already does) and the Shopify storefront de-indexed with 301s to `/store/products/[handle]` | **INFERENCE: the right answer.** One hostname, one entity, one catalogue; `productJsonLd` already emits the markup. Cost: replicate the 301s, and lose Shopify's UCP/`agents.md` surface unless re-implemented |

⚖️ Whichever is chosen, **`NEXT_PUBLIC_SITE_URL` must be set explicitly at launch.**
`lib/site-url.ts` falls back to `VERCEL_PROJECT_PRODUCTION_URL`; if the domain assignment
lags, canonicals silently point at a `.vercel.app` host that `next.config.ts` is
simultaneously serving `X-Robots-Tag: noindex`. Canonical pointing at a noindexed host is
a quiet, total de-indexing.

**And check the Search Console generative-AI control on *every* property involved**
([§2.2](#22-the-one-genuinely-new-actionable-lever-the-search-console-generative-ai-control)) —
inheritance makes a multi-property domain exactly the situation where an *Exclude* hides.

### 7.2 The sitemap is missing most of the site

`app/sitemap.ts` emits 10 static URLs plus conditional blog/area/FAQ entries. Missing,
all indexable with canonicals and no `noindex`:

- `/store/products`
- `/store/products/[handle]` — **all 76 product pages**
- `/builds/[slug]` — `/builds` is listed, its children are not
- `/fleet`, `/events`, `/events/[id]`, `/offers`, `/links`, `/privacy`

→ **Fix:** add a `contentEntries`-style async block calling `getStorefrontCatalog()` and
mapping `/store/products/${handle}`, **filtered by `isSafeToPromote()`** so the DIY Kit is
never submitted to Google. Add build slugs the way `loadPosts()` already works.

### 7.3 The engine-code layer is crawl-blocked

`app/robots.ts` disallows `/*?*`. The intent — stop facet crawling — is defensible. What
it actually blocks:

```
/store/products?platform=duramax          ← "Shop Duramax parts", the platform page's main CTA
/store/products?platform=duramax&gen=…    ← all 19 generation links
/store/products?category=turbo            ← PART_LINES on the homepage
/store/products?category=fuel             ← PART_LINES on the homepage
```

So the homepage's parts section, the platform pages' primary commercial CTA, and the
entire 19-generation navigation are uncrawlable. The engine codes exist in `lib/site.ts`
as strings on cards and nowhere else.

**Fix, in order:**
1. **Give the 19 generations real URLs** — `/duramax/lb7`, `/powerstroke/6-0`,
   `/cummins/5-9-24v`, … Hand-written, one per generation, each with that generation's
   actual known failure modes and the parts the shop stocks. Nest under the platform page.
2. **Replace `?category=` / `?platform=` navigation with path segments**
   (`/store/parts/turbochargers`, `/store/duramax`) so the store is crawlable without a
   blanket disallow.
3. **Then narrow `Disallow: /*?*`** to genuinely infinite parameters (`sort`, `q`, `all`)
   rather than all query strings.

Remember `robots.txt` prevents *crawling*, not *indexing* — a blocked URL can still be
indexed from links, URL-only and snippet-less. Worse for §2: **a page that is not
snippet-eligible is not eligible for AI features at all**
([§2.1](#21-what-google-actually-says--and-it-cuts-against-most-of-what-the-industry-sells)).

### 7.4 The schema graph has no identity

`app/layout.tsx` emits an `AutoRepair` with **no `@id`**.
`lib/marketing/content/seo-schema.ts:8` then emits, on every `Service` page, a **second,
inline `AutoRepair`** as `provider` — also with no `@id`, a different property set, and
`telephone: BUSINESS.phoneDisplay` (`"(843) 995-9252"`) where the root layout uses
`"+1-843-995-9252"`.

Each platform page therefore publishes **two unlinked `AutoRepair` entities with
inconsistent phone formatting**. That is the opposite of the entity clarity that
[§2.6](#26-what-actually-makes-this-business-citable--inference-reasoned) and the box at
the top of this document say is the highest-value work available.

→ **Fix:** give the business `"@id": "https://luckydiesel.com/#business"`; have every
`Service`, `BlogPosting` and `Product` reference it as `provider: { "@id": "…#business" }`;
normalise the phone to E.164 everywhere. A small diff in two files.

⚠️ **Be precise about why, because `@id` is not a Google requirement.** Google *removed*
`@id` from the LocalBusiness documentation in July 2021 — changelog: *"Removed the `@id`
property from the Local business documentation. The `url` property alone is a sufficient
identifier to understand the local business."* So the value here is **not** "Google asks
for `@id`."

The value is that the repo currently emits **two contradictory descriptions of the same
business on the same page**, and no amount of `url` fixes that. `@id` is the mechanism
that collapses them into one node — which is exactly what `lowcountrydiag.com` does
([§7.6](#76-what-the-competitor-two-towns-over-is-doing)). The deliverable is *one
consistent business entity per page*; `@id` is how you get there, and normalising the
phone is half the win on its own.

### 7.4b Which schema types actually produce a SERP feature in 2026

Useful for deciding where to spend effort, and for not over-claiming internally.

| Type | Google doc? | SERP feature in 2026? |
|---|---|---|
| `AutoRepair` / `LocalBusiness` | ✅ | **Partly** — knowledge-panel details. `priceRange` is live and **not deprecated** (docs: *"This field must be shorter than 100 characters. If it's 100 characters or longer, Google won't show a price range"* — present tense). No stars from own reviews |
| `Product` + `Offer` | ✅ | **Yes**, but only on pages where a shopper can actually purchase: *"Only pages where a shopper can purchase a product are eligible for merchant listing experiences"*. Merchant Center is **not** a prerequisite |
| `BreadcrumbList` | ✅ | **Yes — desktop only.** Changelog Jan 2025: *"breadcrumbs… only appear on desktop search results, not mobile"* |
| `VideoObject` | ✅ | **Yes** — video results, Video mode, Images, Discover |
| `ImageObject` (licence metadata) | ✅ | **Yes** — Licensable badge in Images |
| `Organization` | ✅ | Disambiguation + logo/knowledge-panel influence. No standalone rich result. Its newer fields (`hasShippingService`, `hasMerchantReturnPolicy`, `hasMemberProgram`) are merchant features, not shop features |
| **`Service`** | ❌ **no Google doc at all** | **No.** Pure comprehension aid |
| `Offer` standalone | ❌ no doc | Only meaningful nested in `Product` |
| `sameAs`, `areaServed`, `knowsAbout`, `makesOffer`, `hasOfferCatalog` | — | Comprehension aids. Google's only statement on `sameAs` is heavily hedged: *"Google **can** make general use of the `sameAs` property… Some of these elements **may** be used to enable future Search features, **if** they are deemed useful."* |
| `FAQPage`, `HowTo` | ❌ removed | **No** |
| `QAPage` | ✅ — but this use case is explicitly invalid | **No.** See [§1](#1-what-is-actually-in-the-repo-verified-not-re-derived) |

**What this means for the repo:** the `Service` schema on `/[platform]` and
`/service-areas/[area]` earns no SERP feature. That is not a reason to remove it — it is
correct, it is comprehension, and it is where `areaServed` lives — but nobody should
expect a ranking effect from it. The features actually available to this site are
`AutoRepair` knowledge-panel details, `BreadcrumbList` (desktop), `Product` on the store,
and — once there is real photography and video — `ImageObject` and `VideoObject`. **Video
is the most under-exploited of these**, and a phone-shot diagnosis walkthrough is within
reach of a shop with no photographer.

Also worth knowing if the shop ever lists a truck for sale: **vehicle listing structured
data was removed on 2025-09-09** — *"These structured data types are no longer shown in
Google Search results."* And on the merchant side, `Car` is not automatically a `Product`
subtype; Google's docs say to co-type: `"@type": ["Product", "Car"]`.

### 7.5 The `Product` schema is not gated on the claims check

`app/(site)/store/products/[handle]/page.tsx:67` calls `productJsonLd(...)`, and
`components/store/product-json-ld.ts:12` gates only on `product.purchasable`. So the DIY
Kit page emits a `Product` + `AggregateOffer` entity — a **structured, machine-readable
offer for sale** — for a listing whose purchasable options are "Race Pipe" and "EGR Kit".
Given [§5.1](#51-the-statute-in-the-words-that-matter), publishing that as plain text is
bad; publishing it as a typed commercial offer submitted to Google is worse.

→ **Fix (one line):** `if (!product.purchasable || !isSafeToPromote(product)) return null;`

**Related, in the same area:**

**`offRoadOnly` is tag-only and the riskiest SKU carries no tag.**
`lib/store/normalize.ts:201`:
```ts
offRoadOnly: product.tags.some((tag) => /off-?road use only|competition use only/i.test(tag)),
```
The DIY Kit's tags are `DIY Kit, Powerstroke, Powerstroke 2011-2019 6.7L`. So
`offRoadOnly === false` for the most exposed product in the catalogue. It is caught only by
the `race_pipe` regex firing on an option value — one regex between the shop and its worst
listing. If a supplier renames the option "Comp Pipe", it ships.
→ **Fix:** also derive `offRoadOnly` from `checkClaims(promotableText(product)).risk === 'fail'`.

**No `robots` directive on product pages at all.** All 76 are indexable with
self-referencing canonicals. There is no equivalent of the thoughtful `noindex` that
`/fitment` gets.
→ **Fix:** `robots: { index: false, follow: true }` where `isSafeToPromote()` is false. The
shop can still sell it; search engines just aren't invited.

**One accidental mitigation, recorded so nobody "fixes" it into a regression:** the AMDP
"emissions off" text survives only inside a `<table>`, and `parseDescription()`
(`lib/store/normalize.ts:107-131`) extracts only `<p>` and `<li>` content. So that string
does **not** currently render on the Next.js page. That is luck, not design — the fallback
at line 124 strips everything including tables, so a supplier who moves that table into a
paragraph publishes "emissions off" on luckydiesel.com.
→ **Fix:** run `checkClaims()` over rendered product text and refuse to render a `fail`.

### 7.7 Next.js 16 App Router specifics not being used

**(a) The 76 product pages are fully dynamic.**

constraint in `docs/IMAGERY.md`.

**(d) No `twitter` metadata block** in `app/layout.tsx`.

**(e) No image sitemap, no `ImageObject`.** Barely matters with seven photographs; will
matter once there are build shots.

**(f) `/*?*` also blocks UTM-tagged landing URLs from crawl** — harmless for ranking,
worth knowing if a campaign URL is ever meant to be indexed.

### 8.1 The one real gap: AI-assistant referrals are invisible

`lib/marketing/core/attribution-touch.ts`:

```ts
TouchSource = 'google' | 'facebook' | 'instagram' | 'tiktok' | 'bing' | 'youtube'
            | 'referral' | 'email' | 'sms' | 'direct' | 'other'
```

No AI-assistant category. Two consequences:

1. `chatgpt.com`, `perplexity.ai`, `claude.ai`, `copilot.microsoft.com` all collapse into
   `referral` / `other`.
2. **Worse: `gemini.google.com` matches `/(^|\.)google\./` and is classified as `google`**
   — Gemini referrals are being counted as Google organic, silently contaminating the one
   number everyone looks at.

This matters more than it looks, because [§3](#3-measurement-implications-of-2-you-need-to-know-before-reading-8)
establishes that **Search Console's AI report gives impressions only, never clicks.**
Referrer classification is the *only* way to see AI-driven traffic at all.

→ **Fix (small, ship before launch or the baseline is lost):** add an `'ai'` `TouchSource`
and `REFERRER_HOSTS` entries **ahead of the generic Google rule**, matching `chatgpt.com`,
`chat.openai.com`, `perplexity.ai`, `gemini.google.com`, `claude.ai`,
`copilot.microsoft.com`, `you.com`.

Related: the bot filter in `lib/marketing/core/capture.ts` is
`/bot|crawl|spider|slurp|preview|facebookexternalhit|embedly|lighthouse/i`. That catches
`GPTBot`, `ClaudeBot`, `PerplexityBot`, `OAI-SearchBot` (all contain "bot") but **not
`Google-Extended`**. Minor — that agent gets an attribution cookie.

## 11. Widely-repeated beliefs that are wrong in 2026

**❌ "Add `FAQPage` schema for rich results."** The FAQ rich result stopped appearing in
Google Search on **7 May 2026**; the documentation was removed in June 2026 and its URL
now 301-redirects to the changelog; Rich Results Test support went in June 2026 and Search
Console API support in August 2026.
[Changelog](https://developers.google.com/search/updates) ·
[current structured data gallery](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/structured-data/search-gallery).
The local competitor still ships it. This repo is right; they are wrong. *(HowTo went the
same way on 2023-09-14, and vehicle listing on 2025-09-09.)*

**❌ "FAQPage is dead, so use `QAPage` instead."** Google's `QAPage` guidelines name *"an
FAQ page written by the site itself with no way for users to submit alternative answers"*
as an **invalid use case**, and say outright *"Don't use `QAPage` markup for FAQ pages."*
This substitution risks a structured-data manual action — strictly worse than the no-op
the repo currently ships.
[Source](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/structured-data/qapage).

**❌ "Add `AggregateRating` to your LocalBusiness for stars."** Google's docs: those
properties are *"only recommended for sites that capture reviews about **other** local
businesses."*
[Source](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/structured-data/local-business).
⚠️ And the mirror-image error: **"self-serving reviews will get you penalised."** They
won't — Google: *"You won't get a manual action just for this."* It is display
suppression. The right reason not to do it is that it does nothing, not that it is
dangerous.

**❌ "`priceRange` is deprecated."** It is not. It is still in Google's recommended
properties with an active constraint (*"must be shorter than 100 characters. If it's 100
characters or longer, Google won't show a price range for the business"* — present tense),
and there is no deprecation entry in the changelog. What *was* removed from that doc is
`@id` (July 2021) and `additionalType` support (August 2022); those get conflated.

**❌ "You need `llms.txt` to be found by AI."** John Mueller: *"FWIW no AI system currently
uses llms.txt"* ([Bluesky](https://bsky.app/profile/johnmu.com/post/3lrshm4gggs2v)). The
2026 guide: *"Google Search itself doesn't use them."* The changelog entry of 2026-06-15
is the most precise statement: these files *"**won't negatively or positively impact your
visibility or rankings**… it's fine if you want to maintain these files for other services
or systems that use them."* Adoption is ~5.6% of the top 10k and most of that is one
Shopify rollout ([study](https://caseyrb.com/blog/state-of-llms-txt-adoption/)). So: not
harmful, not useful, not a priority. `luckydiesel.com` almost certainly already has one
from Shopify.

**❌ "There's special schema for AI Overviews."** Google lists over-focusing on structured
data as a **myth**, and the only causal study found **no uplift and a −4.6% AIO decline**
(n=1,885 treated, ~4,000 controls, difference-in-differences).
[Google guide](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/ai-optimization-guide) ·
[Ahrefs](https://ahrefs.com/blog/schema-ai-citations/). Ship schema for rich-result
eligibility and NAP hygiene, not as an AI lever.

**❌ "Block `Google-Extended` to opt out of AI Overviews."** Wrong surface.
Google-Extended covers *other* Google systems. AI Overviews and AI Mode are controlled by
Googlebot robots.txt, the snippet directives, and the new **Search Console generative-AI
control**. [Source](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/ai-features) ·
[control](https://support.google.com/webmasters/answer/16908024?hl=en).

**❌ "Build pages targeting fan-out queries."** Google names that exact practice as
violating the **scaled content abuse** spam policy, in the same document that explains
fan-out.
[Source](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/ai-optimization-guide).

**❌ "AI Overviews have killed the local pack."** The best measured local study finds
local-intent queries return a **local pack 93%** of the time and an **AI Overview 15%**.
They are complementary surfaces split by intent.
[Whitespark](https://whitespark.ca/blog/case-study-the-prevalence-of-ai-overviews-in-local-search/).

**❌ "Blocking GPTBot removes you from ChatGPT."** It removes you from *training*, not from
ChatGPT search citations. **OAI-SearchBot** is the one that costs you citations, and
OpenAI documents them as independent.
[Source](https://developers.openai.com/api/docs/bots).

**❌ "The January 2026 DOJ memo made deletes legal."** It ended *criminal* prosecutions as
a matter of prosecutorial discretion. **DOJ expressly said civil enforcement continues**,
the statute is unchanged, civil penalties are **$5,911 per violation** (40 C.F.R. §19.4,
90 FR 1375), and the five-year civil limitations period means content published today is
live evidence into 2031. This is the most dangerous misconception in the diesel industry
right now.

**❌ "'Off-road use only' protects the seller."** EPA's Tampering Policy says it "does not
address… aftermarket parts **purportedly** manufactured or sold for that purpose" —
i.e. no protection offered. In practice the disclaimer helps prove the statute's "knows or
should know" element. The RPM Act has never been enacted.
[Tampering Policy](https://www.epa.gov/sites/production/files/2020-12/documents/epatamperingpolicy-enforcementpolicyonvehicleandenginetampering.pdf).

**❌ "South Carolina has no emissions testing, so it doesn't matter."** Federal law does
not depend on a state I/M programme, and every cited enforcement case arose in a
non-CARB/limited-I/M state. Plus **SC §56-5-5020 and §56-5-5030** address smoke directly,
the latter as a misdemeanour.

**❌ "Block query strings in robots.txt to control crawl."** `robots.txt` prevents
*crawling*, not *indexing*. And here the blanket rule blocks the site's own navigation.
Use `noindex` for indexing, path segments for structure, and reserve disallow for
genuinely infinite parameter spaces.

**❌ "Programmatic location pages are free traffic."** Google's spam policy names
"multiple domain names targeting specific regions/cities funneling to one page" and
"substantially similar pages" as **doorway abuse** (policy updated 2026-08-28).
[Source](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/essentials/spam-policies).
`seo-guard.ts` is the right defence; don't route around it.

## 12. Limitations and open questions

- **No keyword tool data.** Ahrefs: `Insufficient plan` on every endpoint including the
  free DR one. Semrush: `no_api_units` (<https://www.semrush.com/mcp-access>). All volume
  and KD in §4 is ESTIMATE. Re-run before any budget decision — priority pulls: DR and
  backlink profile for `charleston-diesel.com`, `lowcountrydiag.com`,
  `palmettodieselandperformance.com`; real volume/KD on the symptom cluster; `serp_features`
  to check AI Overview exposure on the symptom terms *before* building the strategy on top
  of them.
- **No AI Overview presence data** for any term — SERP-feature endpoints were plan-blocked.
- **No Search Console data.** The site has never been indexed; there is no baseline.
  Everything about current performance is inferred from competitor SERPs.
- **Every AI-citation study cited is vendor-published.** There is no independent or
  academic work on AI citation selection. Even the best (Ahrefs) has a commercial interest
  in the answer.
- **The best local-specific study is May 2025** — 540 queries, 3 cities, 6 verticals,
  researchers out-of-market. Its intent-split finding is probably durable; its trigger
  rates are likely stale. An in-market replication for Charleston diesel would be a few
  hundred queries and far more persuasive than a national average.
- **The DOJ January 2026 memo was verified through multiple news outlets, not the memo
  itself.** ⚖️
- **The SEMA Certified-Emissions EPA advisory opinion (July 2026)** is sourced from SEMA's
  own FAQ, not from the opinion. ⚖️ Verify before citing publicly.
- **DPF cleaning safety is INFERENCE**, not a cited EPA position. No EPA document addresses
  it by name. ⚖️
- **SC I/M status has no first-party source** — the SCDMV page 404'd. And "SC DHEC" in
  `docs/DISCOVERY.md` is likely a stale agency name (now SC DES after the 2024
  reorganisation). ⚖️
- **CARB EO format:** `normalizeEoNumber()` assumes `D-123-45`. The `arb.ca.gov` pages I
  tried returned 404. Confirm against a current CARB source before it gates anything
  user-facing.
- **Spam-policy section renames are undated.** The headings are now *Doorway abuse*, *Site
  reputation policy* and *Thin affiliation* (observed 2026-09-18), but the renames were
  never announced in the changelog and the Wayback API was unreachable, so I cannot say
  when they changed. The anchors (`#doorways`, `#site-reputation`, `#thin-affiliate-pages`)
  are unchanged.
- **Quality Rater Guidelines are evidence of intent, not a ranking mechanism.** Google
  states rater ratings *"don't directly influence ranking."* §6.1 cites them for how
  Google trains humans to recognise paraphrased location pages, not as a ranking factor.
- **No Google guidance on city/location pages exists in 2025 or 2026.** A full check of
  the changelog, the blog index and the 182-page rater guidelines returns zero hits for
  "city page" or "location page". Any claim that "Google said in 2025 that city pages are
  fine / not fine" is secondary-source invention.
- **Danny Sullivan stepped down as Search Liaison on 2025-08-01** (trade press). Any
  "@searchliaison said…" citation dated after that is a different team — date it
  carefully before quoting it at anyone.
- **No precedent found** for a shop successfully marketing "emissions repair" as an SEO
  strategy. [§5.6](#56-what-the-shop-can-legitimately-rank-for) is a reasoned hypothesis
  with a clear legal basis, not a proven playbook.
- **Methodological warning worth recording:** during this research an automated page
  summariser returned **fabricated quotations** attributed to the EPA Tampering Policy.
  They were caught by downloading and extracting the PDF directly. Every EPA quotation in
  §5 comes from text extracted from the source document, and the Lucky Diesel Performance
  settlement was downloaded and extracted by me specifically to verify it. **Treat any
  AI-generated quotation of a legal source as unverified until checked against the
  original.**
- ⚖️ **Nothing in §5 is legal advice.** B5, B6 and B7 need counsel, not an SEO.
